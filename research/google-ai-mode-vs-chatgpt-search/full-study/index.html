What “full study” means here

Research attribution. Research conducted by SXF Research. SXF Research owns the research design and question selection, and documented and published this original study. Google AI Mode and ChatGPT Search are the evaluated platforms, not the study authors or publishers.

This appendix publishes the complete wording of all 14 prompts, each first-run preliminary score and the editorial review notes, with primary-source starting links. A user submitted 28 original answers during the research conversation. The spreadsheet archives some of them in normalized or abridged form, and the available excerpt file is not a verified verbatim transcript. The full raw 28-answer archive has not been independently reconstructed.

ChatGPT originally graded both tools, including itself, and awarded itself full marks on every item. There has been no independent blind review, no additional repeat runs and no statistical validation. Do not use these numbers as a general accuracy benchmark.

Study methodology

Research responsibilities. SXF Research selected the 14 questions for this pilot and documented and published the results; the 14 prompts were submitted once to each platform, and 28 first-run answers were collected. ChatGPT assisted in preliminary scoring of both tools, including itself.

Collection. The same 14 English prompts were manually run once in Google AI Mode and once in ChatGPT Search. The user pasted the Google answer first and ChatGPT answer second for each task. Questions covered recent developments, official sources, peer-reviewed science, regulations, arithmetic, Python, SEO, and crawler controls.

Sampling. SXF Research deliberately chose questions; no randomly sampled real-search query set was used. The responses were reviewed on November 5, 2026. Exact timestamps and device/search settings for each original run were not recorded; the date is a study-session context, not independently logged per-response execution time.

Score rule. Factual accuracy 40%; sources 30%; freshness and relevance 20%; clarity/usefulness 10%. Each dimension received a 0–5 score, and the total out of 100 was 8×accuracy + 6×sources + 4×freshness + 2×usefulness. Fractional dimension ratings were permitted.

Independence. ChatGPT assigned the preliminary scores for Google AI Mode and ChatGPT Search, including itself. This was unblinded scoring by a competing product, not independent human review or independent validation. In particular, the provisional 100/100 ChatGPT mean is an artifact of that scoring process and not validated evidence of universal perfection. Scores and examples may change after neutral claim-level re-evaluation.

Original descriptive figures. Google AI Mode: 1296 total points, averaging 92.57/100. ChatGPT Search: 1400 points, averaging 100/100. Six first-pass ties, eight provisional ChatGPT leads, zero independent inter-rater reliability measurements.

A high-quality future study would add multiple fresh runs per prompt, preserve exact raw output and citations, counterbalance tool order, record interface and time, and conduct at least two blinded independent ratings. None of that is implied by this first-run audit.

Evidence review: the highest-impact observed examples

T11: a cited but mis-selected API price tier

The first Google answer used $60 / $300 per million GPT-6 Astra tokens instead of the Standard $10 / $50 per million requested. For the specified 2 million input and 0.5 million output tokens, that created a $270 estimate where the Standard-token calculation was $45. Its arithmetic was internally consistent for the wrong tier. This example supports checking pricing context, not any claim about typical error rate. Original reference: OpenAI API pricing.

T06: biosignatures are not confirmed life

Both tools rejected the K2-18 b viral claim. The editorial review noted differences in distinguishing methane and other atmospheric inference from tentative potential biosignatures, independent reanalyses, and non-biological alternatives. Starting references: NASA Science and peer-reviewed reanalysis.

T13–T14: search visibility is not AI model training

Googlebot, Google-Extended, OAI-SearchBot and GPTBot have different control meanings; allowing search discovery is not identical to granting training permission. Neither crawler permission nor a citation-rich answer guarantees search placement. Starting sources: Google Search Central, Google Crawlers and OpenAI Bots.

What these results can and cannot tell us

These 14 paired tasks can illustrate source selection errors, scientific uncertainty and the need to check technical guidance. They do not identify overall population error rates for either product. Without repeated testing we cannot measure stability. Without independent blind graders we cannot confidently separate tool quality from evaluator preference. Without verified verbatim logs we cannot claim full third-party reproducibility of every original answer.

The transparent next step is to freeze raw outputs, conduct blinded evaluation and add repeat sessions as a separately versioned study. This page should be updated with those records rather than silently revising the original pilot.
