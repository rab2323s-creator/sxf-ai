DIRECT ANSWER

Can you use open-source or open-weight AI models commercially?

Often yes, but never decide from a license tag alone. Check which terms govern the exact weight release, whether the grant covers your commercial activity, what notices or attribution must accompany redistributed artifacts, and whether additional acceptable-use policies, trademarks, data rights or regulation apply. MIT and Apache 2.0 are permissive licenses, while custom model terms must be read individually.

A release being free to download does not mean the complete AI system satisfies the Open Source AI Definition or that your intended use has been legally cleared. Record provenance, obtain a human review and repeat it when changing models or business activity.

LEGAL & ENGINEERING GUIDE

What is AI model licensing—and can you use open-source AI commercially?

AI model licensing determines which rights a particular legal instrument grants over a specified model artifact, subject to its conditions. A company may be using an open-weight model for internal inference, offering a paid SaaS using that model, reselling access to a hosted inference API or distributing a modified checkpoint. These are not automatically the same contractual act.

Many permissive licenses allow commercial exploitation—but downloadable weights, a model-card license tag or an “open source” label do not independently prove permission for every activity. You must identify the exact released weights, version, governing license text and any incorporated acceptable-use terms, then evaluate the actual commercial deployment. See the SXF Open-Source and Open-Weight Model Guide for current model choices; this guide instead focuses on operational evidence and obligations.

Direct answer

For a commercial AI product: pin the weight artifact and license version; verify commercial, hosted-service, modification and distribution grants; preserve notices; review custom policies and independent laws; record legal review before deployment. This page supplies an audit framework, not a legal clearance.

Neither MIT nor Apache-2.0 automatically gives rights to third-party datasets, personal data, unrelated trademarks or all model artifacts. A repository may contain differently licensed weights, source code, tokenizers or examples. For the actual texts see MIT and Apache 2.0.

Important nuance

A license-comparison table cannot establish whether a named model artifact is actually governed by that license. Confirm the complete license document, any model-specific addendum and the released revision before concluding anything about commercial use.

APACHE-2.0 COMPLIANCE

Apache 2.0 AI models: commercial use, NOTICE and patent clauses

Apache License 2.0 provides a permissive copyright grant and express patent terms, while imposing distribution conditions. Section 4 addresses reproducing the license, marking modified files, retaining required attribution notices and carrying applicable NOTICE contents when the original work includes a NOTICE file. Section 6 does not grant general rights to use licensors' trade names or trademarks.

For a downloadable model checkpoint, the reviewer must ask whether Apache-2.0 covers the weights themselves, the repository's code, or both. If you redistribute a modified model, record what was changed and package the license/required notices correctly. This is separate from the technical question of which inference server or quantization format you use.

LEGAL & ENGINEERING GUIDE

MIT licensed AI models: what actually travels with a commercial product

The MIT License grants broad rights over covered material, including use, copy, modification, distribution, sublicensing and sale, subject to inclusion of the copyright and permission notice in copies or substantial portions. It is not a warranty or guarantee of ownership over every artifact a project bundles.

For commercial AI deployment, retain the actual licensor notice and record which materials are covered. Hosted inference may not involve giving users weights, while distributing an on-device model package does. Separate rights in training datasets, third-party components, branding and product claims remain separate.

LEGAL & ENGINEERING GUIDE

Custom LLM licenses: Llama, Gemma and version-specific usage conditions

Model-specific agreements can be designed for broad commercial adoption while imposing conditions absent from plain MIT or Apache 2.0. A contract may distinguish distributing the model itself from making model functionality available as a service, or incorporate an acceptable-use policy. The text and release version control.

Custom-licensed LLM examples: document checklist, not legal approvals
ExamplePrimary documents to inspectWhat must not be assumed
Meta Llama 4Llama 4 Community License Agreement and associated Acceptable Use PolicyThat a historical Llama license, general “commercial use allowed” claim or an older threshold applies unchanged
Google GemmaThe exact release's Gemma Terms of Use and referenced prohibited-use termsThat the word “open” or a repository badge removes custom conditions
Other custom modelModel release agreement, incorporated policies, license notice and version historyThat a license tag in a model registry captures the complete contract
Permissively licensed alternativeExact weight license, NOTICE and model/source cardThat the training set and system as a whole meet the OSI Open Source AI Definition

Llama 4 example: Meta's published Llama 4 agreement describes a monthly-active-user scale provision for organizations above a defined threshold and imposes naming/attribution conditions; those details need checking against the original text and your legal entities. Avoid generalizing the threshold to all Llama releases or to unrelated Meta systems. Read the Llama 4 agreement and its use policy before forming a deployment conclusion.

Version dependency

A model family name is not a governing contract. Capture the exact model and license release, effective agreement, terms accepted and authorized commercial activity, then have a qualified reviewer check the relevant provisions.

Do not assume that access to a paid API means the customer has received model weights, or conversely that an API is outside all custom-license obligations. The definition of “distribution,” “service,” “derivative work,” “model materials” and “affiliate” varies by contract and legal setting.

LEGAL & ENGINEERING GUIDE

Fine-tuning, LoRA, quantization and model redistribution: what changes?

  1. Track the original weight artifact: canonical source, revision, hash and governing license.
  2. Track transformation provenance: fine-tuning dataset rights, adapters, merged weights, quantization process and changed files.
  3. Record new package contents: converted tensors, tokenizer, config, runtime code and accompanying model cards; licenses can differ.
  4. Review redistribution grants: where the modified weights go, which legal entities receive them, and whether extra obligations arise.
  5. Preserve obligations: license copies, notices, copyright statements, relevant NOTICE contents and change indicators.
  6. Version the final decision: a new upstream checkpoint or model-card change requires renewed inspection.

The engineering details of memory reduction are in LLM Quantization Explained; deployment mechanics are in How to Run AI Models Locally. Here the issue is licensing the original and redistributed artifacts, not benchmarking their speed.

LEGAL & ENGINEERING GUIDE

EU AI Act and open-source model exemptions: what companies must distinguish

Do not assume “open source” means “EU AI Act exempt.” The EU AI Act distinguishes actors and obligations—including providers of general-purpose AI models, deployers and systems with particular risk profiles. Open-source-related exceptions are conditional and do not remove every obligation; special rules can continue to apply to general-purpose AI models with systemic risk. The applicable dates, guidance and any later amendments should be checked for the precise model and business role.

For a real EU-facing launch, begin with the actual EU AI Act regulation and the European Commission's guidance; document whether your organization is merely deploying an already-provided model or creates a legally relevant new system or model provider role. Assess obligations separately from license permissions.

Role and timing matter

This guide is not a substitute for EU regulatory advice. The relevant risk category, provider/deployer role, open-source conditions, systemic-risk designation and compliance dates must be established on the date of the actual release.

LEGAL & ENGINEERING GUIDE

Step-by-step AI model licensing audit for real deployments

  1. Define use: identify the operating company, customers, product, territory and whether weights leave your systems.
  2. Pin artifact: exact canonical release, revision, SHA256 and all components in the deployment bundle.
  3. Read the full grants: MIT, Apache-2.0 or custom text as applied to each relevant component—not repository metadata alone.
  4. Collect incorporated policies: restricted uses, versioned acceptable-use terms, thresholds, branding and third-party conditions.
  5. Prepare notices: copyright, LICENSE, NOTICE when applicable, modification labels and downstream files.
  6. Separate legal layers: data rights, privacy, industry regulation, export, trademarks and the EU AI Act where relevant.
  7. Get human sign-off: record reviewer, decision scope, unresolved questions and evidence references; never “auto-approve” from tags.
  8. Gate deployment: block or escalate launch for missing mandatory evidence or unapproved high-risk conditions.
  9. Recheck on change: new model or checkpoint, weights redistribution, country expansion, modified policy or new customers.

LEGAL & ENGINEERING GUIDE

Ten AI model license mistakes that create hidden commercial risk

  1. Treating a public weights download as a blanket commercial license.
  2. Conflating a permissive repository code license with the released checkpoint's license.
  3. Assuming the “open-source AI” label satisfies the system-level OSI definition.
  4. Assuming a Llama license applies identically across all Llama versions.
  5. Distributing quantized weights without checking original terms and notices.
  6. Copying “MIT” or “Apache” into a spreadsheet without the actual source and license text.
  7. Ignoring model-specific acceptable-use terms incorporated by reference.
  8. Using a model brand in marketing without checking trademark constraints.
  9. Confusing weight license permissions with AI Act, privacy or training-data obligations.
  10. Publishing automated “legally approved” badges without qualified human review.

LEGAL & ENGINEERING GUIDE

Frequently asked questions about commercial AI model licenses

Can I use an open-source AI model commercially?

Often, but you must inspect the exact weight artifact and governing terms. Permissive licenses such as MIT and Apache-2.0 can grant commercial permissions subject to conditions. A public download or 'open' marketing label is not sufficient evidence.

Are open weights the same as open-source AI?

No. Publicly available weights alone do not establish the Open Source AI Definition's freedoms and preferred form for modification; related code and training-data information also matter.

Does an Apache 2.0 AI model allow commercial use?

Apache-2.0 is a permissive license generally allowing commercial use and redistribution subject to its terms. Confirm it actually governs the weights and check notices, modification obligations, patents and third-party rights.

Can I commercially use a model under the MIT License?

MIT is permissive and generally permits commercial use of the licensed material while requiring the copyright and permission notices to be included in copies or substantial portions. Scope and other rights require verification.

Can I sell an API powered by Llama?

You must review the exact Llama release's custom license and acceptable-use policy for the hosted service, threshold rules, branding and any restrictions. Do not infer rights from the name 'Llama' alone.

Is hosting model weights behind an API the same as redistributing them?

Not necessarily. Hosted inference, delivering weights to customers and distributing a modified checkpoint can be distinct activities; contract definitions and applicable law determine relevant conditions.

What changes when I fine-tune or quantize open weights?

You need to check permissions and obligations covering modifications, new artifacts, attribution, redistributed checkpoint packaging, third-party dependencies and any derivative use restrictions.

Is a Hugging Face license tag enough for legal compliance?

No. A license field is useful metadata, but the actual version-specific legal text, model card, referenced policies, provenance and artifact scope must be reviewed.

Does open-source licensing exempt an AI provider from the EU AI Act?

Do not assume a blanket exemption. Certain open-source-related exemptions are role- and condition-specific, and some obligations can remain, particularly for systemic-risk models. Consult the EU AI Act and current Commission guidance for your role and release.

Does an AI model license cover training data, trademark and privacy?

Not automatically. Training-data rights, third-party material, trademark/branding, privacy, consumer law and sector obligations can arise independently.

How do I audit an AI model license before production?

Pin the weights' revision and source, collect governing licenses and incorporated terms, map the intended commercial activity, prepare notices and third-party inventories, resolve jurisdictional requirements, and obtain human sign-off.

Can an AI license checker automatically approve a model?

No general automated checker can reliably replace contractual and legal analysis. Automated evidence checks can flag missing documents but cannot prove permission, resolve ambiguous restrictions or replace a qualified review.
