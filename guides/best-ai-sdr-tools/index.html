Best AI SDR Tools for 2027: AI Sales Agents Compared by Pricing, Automation and Reliability

SXF / AI Research Desk · 2027 buyer's guide · Public sources reviewed November 28, 2026 · Documentation-based comparison, not a hands-on benchmark · USD

The best AI SDR tool is not necessarily the one that sends the most messages. It is the one that finds relevant buyers, uses defensible evidence to personalize outreach, respects contact restrictions and produces qualified meetings at a cost your business can sustain.

Eight platforms illustrate how different this category has become. Some delegate outbound prospecting and follow-ups to an AI sales agent. Others help human sellers research accounts and execute campaigns. One specializes in converting inbound interest. Treating all eight as interchangeable digital sales employees would make their pricing and capabilities misleading.

Quick answer: which AI SDR tool should you choose?

  • AiSDR is worth evaluating for teams that want outbound automation with a published per-person usage rate and campaign approval.
  • Salesforge Agent Frank is a candidate for teams that want delegated outbound with a choice between autonomous and reviewed operation, and can manage the additional sending infrastructure.
  • Artisan Ava and 11x Outbound belong on the shortlist for organizations considering a more extensive outbound agent deployment. Compare written quotes and governance requirements before committing.
  • RegieGO and Apollo are sensible starting points for sellers who want AI-assisted prospecting rather than an immediate replacement for a human SDR.
  • Amplemarket Duo is worth exploring when account signals, seller workflows and engagement need to work within a single sales platform.
  • Qualified Piper is designed for inbound qualification and conversion rather than a cold-outbound-first workflow.

These are editorial use-case matches, not rankings derived from measured conversion rates. There is no independently established reliability winner in this comparison.

What is an AI SDR?

An AI sales development representative (AI SDR) is software that performs some of the early sales-development work: identifying prospective customers, researching accounts, drafting relevant messages, coordinating follow-ups, qualifying interest and routing opportunities to a seller.

The label describes a workflow, not a standardized autonomy level. Three categories matter when buying AI SDR software:

CategoryWhat the software doesWhat the human still owns
Prospecting assistantSuggests accounts, gathers data, writes draftsTarget selection, campaign execution, customer conversations
Outbound sales agentExecutes an approved prospecting and outreach process; may interpret repliesPolicy, escalation, quality control, negotiation and deal ownership
Inbound sales agentResponds to existing interest, qualifies and routes buyersComplex questions, exceptions and the sales relationship

Traditional sales automation follows predetermined triggers and templates. An AI sales agent can also choose actions based on context, but that discretion introduces new failure modes. An agent can choose an irrelevant buyer, misinterpret a company's news or confidently make an unauthorized promise. For the broader distinction, see SXF's general AI agent buyer's guide.

How SXF evaluates AI SDR software

We selected eight platforms to represent different purchasing models and sales workflows. The shortlist is not an exhaustive market census. Evidence was taken from public provider pricing pages, technical documentation, product references and help centers reviewed on November 28, 2026.

Our evaluation framework asks nine questions:

  1. Targeting: Can the product implement a precise ideal customer profile (ICP), including exclusions?
  2. Evidence: Does the personalization have a traceable and current source?
  3. Execution: Which steps, channels and replies can actually be automated?
  4. Integration: Which CRM records can be read or changed, and can outcomes be exported?
  5. Human control: Can an operator approve consequential actions and pause the agent immediately?
  6. Deliverability: Are invalid addresses, suppression requests and sending limits respected?
  7. Economics: What is the contractual unit, and what costs sit outside it?
  8. Privacy: What data is collected, retained, shared, deleted or used for model training?
  9. Evidence quality: Is the capability documented, independently observed, or still unknown?

Evidence boundary: public documentation can establish that a feature or price is advertised. It cannot establish real-world accuracy, deliverability, meeting conversion, the effectiveness of security controls or compliance in a particular deployment. We have not run live campaigns, obtained vendor test accounts or measured comparative product outcomes for this article. No numerical performance scores are assigned.

Individual product profiles

AiSDR

Best fit: B2B teams that want a usage-metered way to explore delegated outbound.

AiSDR's Ami agent uses prospect research and signals to plan and execute campaigns. Its public documentation describes email and LinkedIn outreach, enrichment, reply handling and HubSpot integration; Salesforce is listed among the team-plan features. Initial campaigns require approval, and the vendor describes a reviewed reply mode.

The public self-service price is $1.50 per person prospected, with daily charging and the option to stop. Larger teams can request volume-based plans. This makes the billing unit easier to understand than a generic AI credit, although the buyer must still define what constitutes a billed person and confirm any channel-specific expenses.

What to test: whether the agent identifies the correct buying role; whether the research genuinely supports each personalization claim; how changes to campaigns are approved; and whether a suppression request stops every subsequent touch. Confirm the CRM tier and the costs of any optional external services.

Official sources: Pricing · Ami product documentation · Product overview

Salesforge Agent Frank

Best fit: Teams looking to delegate outbound while retaining a choice of operating mode.

Agent Frank is the agent option within the Salesforge ecosystem, distinct from the company's human-operated sequencing subscription. Its documentation describes prospect sourcing, personalized outreach, autonomous operation and a co-pilot configuration. Forge documentation also describes CRM connections.

The agent-specific page displays $499 per month on annual billing for 1,000 active contacts; its FAQ lists $599 per month with quarterly billing. Active contacts are prospects being processed concurrently, not a fixed count of new people reached each month. The provider says the agent subscription does not cover the required email infrastructure.

Pricing discrepancy: the general pricing page also displays a $416 annual-billing monthly calculator result for 1,000 active contacts alongside a $499 headline. These values cannot be reconciled from the public pages alone. We use the agent-specific $499 as the reference, not as a guaranteed quote. Ask Salesforge to confirm the effective rate, infrastructure fees and minimum commitment in writing.

What to test: approval behavior for initial messages and replies separately, CRM write-back, how active contacts are counted, and whether mailbox or lead-data costs change as volume increases. Do not confuse the standard Salesforge trial with access to Frank.

Official sources: Agent Frank · Agent help · Forge stack integration · Trial scope

Artisan Ava

Best fit: Organizations that want outbound research, engagement and meeting booking with configurable controls.

Artisan positions Ava as its outbound BDR and Aaron as its separate inbound SDR. Ava's documented workflow covers buyer identification, tailored outreach, campaign handling and meeting scheduling. The provider's technical launch description identifies approval checkpoints and CRM write controls. Its pricing page lists Salesforce and HubSpot synchronization and enterprise add-ons that include audit logs.

Price: Not publicly disclosed. Request an itemized quote for the outbound agent rather than assuming that the inbound agent, enterprise controls, or advertising expenditure is included. Confirm whether credits, contacted people, seats and sending infrastructure determine the final bill.

What to test: read-only CRM configuration, prohibited-field writes, escalation of pricing questions and the agent's handling of conflicting account information. Verify exactly which plan includes the controls your organization requires.

Official sources: Plans and pricing · Ava 2.0 architecture and controls

11x Outbound (Alice)

Best fit: Sales organizations prepared to assess a larger annual deployment with managed outbound infrastructure.

11x documents Alice as an outbound agent that researches prospective customers and generates personalized outreach. Its separate inbound product serves a different workflow. Alice's personalization documentation describes manual review, approval and automatic operation. The setup guide identifies Salesforce and HubSpot as CRM options.

The Growth plan starts at $36,000 a year, billed annually, and lists 2,000 new prospects per month and up to five users. The provider lists managed mailboxes, onboarding and CRM synchronization within this plan. The higher Pro and Enterprise plans are quote based.

What to test: whether all needed channels are included in the quoted tier, when approvals are required, how reply escalation works and what happens to contact history when the contract ends. A substantial annual commitment makes a written acceptance plan especially important.

Official sources: Outbound pricing · Alice overview · Personalization and approval · Setup

RegieGO

Best fit: Individual sellers and sales teams that want AI-assisted research, prospecting and calling while retaining direct control of their work.

Regie.ai now presents RegieGO as an agentic prospecting workspace. Its public plans include account research, contact enrichment, message drafting and agents. Pro adds a parallel dialer; the platform offers a built-in CRM or connections to HubSpot and Salesforce. Its research documentation describes draft review and configurable approval before sending.

The free plan offers a one-time allowance of 1,000 credits per person. Pro costs $41 per month on annual billing ($49 monthly) with 5,000 monthly credits. Team costs $125 per seat per month annually ($150 monthly), with a two-seat minimum and 15,000 monthly credits per seat. Different activities consume credits differently.

What to test: the credits required for a representative account research and contact enrichment job, the quality of call preparation and the time a seller still spends on manual review. A low entry subscription should not be interpreted as the price of a fully autonomous SDR.

Official sources: RegieGO pricing · Research and approvals · Credit calculator

Amplemarket Duo

Best fit: Sales teams that prioritize intent signals and a coordinated rep-led engagement platform, with optional autonomous outreach.

Amplemarket combines contact data, account signals, enrichment and engagement features. Duo Copilot surfaces relevant leads and recommends outreach; vendor training documentation also describes an autopilot option that removes the manual review step for supported sequences. Calling and social tasks exist within the broader platform; do not assume Duo autonomously handles every channel.

The Startup plan advertises $600 per month on an annual term with two users and Duo Copilot included. Growth and Elite use custom quotes. Confirm how contact allowances, enrichment, additional users and any advanced voice functionality are metered.

What to test: signal freshness, whether the recommended prospect matches the signal, duplicate prevention and the boundary between human-reviewed recommendations and automatic scheduling. Ask for the data provenance of each recommendation.

Official sources: Pricing · Duo training · Autopilot mode

Qualified Piper

Best fit: Businesses with an existing stream of inbound website visitors or leads that need qualification and routing.

Piper is an inbound AI SDR. The vendor lists website interactions, email conversations, meeting scheduling and multimodal interaction. Salesforce integration and routing are central to the offering; higher plans add capabilities such as agent handoff and API access.

Price: Not publicly disclosed. Plans use capacity measures that differ from outbound agents, including monthly website sessions and active email contacts. A visitor-to-meeting workflow is economically different from finding cold prospects.

What to test: whether the agent answers only from authorized product knowledge, recognizes unsupported pricing questions, routes the buyer correctly and checks calendar availability. Request the actual tier-specific approval, audit, and human-takeover controls; a per-message approval mechanism was not established in the public material reviewed.

Official source: Qualified pricing and plan capabilities

Apollo

Best fit: Teams that need a prospect database, research and engagement tools with agents that support a seller's decisions.

Apollo is a broader sales intelligence and execution platform, rather than a direct equivalent to a managed outbound digital worker. Its agent documentation describes prospect recommendations, sequence suggestions and background research, with the seller able to review results. Some activities are credit-metered and the AI assistant documents approval controls over credit expenditure. Credit approval is not the same thing as approval to send a message.

Apollo's live pricing page confirms a free plan, Basic, Professional and custom tiers. A separate official pricing explainer lists Basic at $49 per user per month annually and Professional at $79. Because the current main page did not expose numeric tier prices in the accessible view, treat the paid figures as vendor reference prices, not independently confirmed checkout prices. Confirm agent access and credit allowances in the actual plan.

What to test: whether contact records are current, how much research consumes credits, whether a seller can trace the rationale for each recommendation, and which agent actions can modify CRM records or send messages without further approval.

AI SDR pricing: calculate the cost per qualified meeting, not the cost per message

The vendors in this comparison meter different things: people contacted, concurrently active contacts, seats, credits, annual prospect allowances or inbound traffic. A credit is not a lead, and a contacted lead is not a qualified sales opportunity.

Before comparing quotes, ask each provider to price the same intended workload and identify the exact unit that triggers additional charges. Include every cost needed to keep that workflow operating:

  • Agent subscription and minimum contract value.
  • Seats, credits, enrichment and additional data sources.
  • Authorized mailbox, domain and channel infrastructure.
  • CRM, integration and support expenses.
  • Human review, reply escalation and error correction.
  • Setup, security review and ongoing operational maintenance.

SXF SDR unit-economics model

Define a qualified held meeting as a meeting that actually occurred and met a pre-agreed buyer-fit and qualification rubric. Count each unique opportunity only once under the selected attribution window. An automatically scheduled meeting that never happens does not qualify.

Let:

  • S = monthly subscription, usage and seat fees.
  • I = monthly data, infrastructure, integration and other recurring expenses not included in S.
  • H = total monthly human oversight hours.
  • R = fully loaded hourly cost of that work.
  • F = one-time implementation expense.
  • N = unique prospects contacted during the measured period.
  • Q = qualified held meetings attributed to those prospects.
Recurring monthly TCO = S + I + H × R
Year-one TCO = 12 × recurring monthly TCO + F
Qualified held meeting rate = Q / N
Cost per qualified held meeting = recurring monthly TCO / Q

For a steady-state monthly comparison that includes setup, add F / 12 to recurring TCO. If Q is zero, report the spend and zero outcomes instead of a misleading zero cost per meeting. When calculating across an entire year, divide total year-one cost by the actual number of qualified held meetings during that year, not a monthly rate extrapolated from a short trial.

Reliability, deliverability, privacy and governance

The worst failure in an AI SDR workflow is not always a missed meeting. It may be a false personalization claim, contact with an opted-out recipient, an unauthorized CRM modification, or a product promise that your sales team cannot honor.

Measure reliability at each decision boundary

MeasurementDefinitionWhy it matters
Target precisionEligible unique prospects / reviewed unique prospectsDetects weak targeting
Personalization error rateDrafts containing at least one unsupported factual claim / reviewed draftsDetects fabricated or stale evidence
Qualified held meeting rateUnique qualified held meetings / unique prospects contactedMeasures useful pipeline, not activity
Human review timeTotal reviewer minutes / reviewed actionsReveals the operational burden
Unauthorized action rateUnauthorized actions / audited attempted actionsTracks control failures
CRM reconciliation rateCorrect verified CRM changes / expected CRM changesConfirms downstream state
Suppression violationsAttempts to contact suppressed recipientsCritical policy failure; investigate every occurrence

Always publish denominators and separate draft-only evaluations from real campaign outcomes. Report results by segment and channel to avoid hiding poorly performing cohorts. See AI Agent Reliability for the broader distinction between capability and dependable execution.

Deliverability is an operating constraint, not a growth hack

A provider may advertise verification, monitoring or mailbox management. None guarantees delivery, recipient engagement or legal permission to contact a person. Configure legitimate sender authentication, keep contact restrictions synchronized, avoid deceptive personalization, and stop campaigns if complaint or bounce patterns indicate a problem.

Google's email sender guidelines explain authentication, spam and unsubscribe requirements for messages to personal Gmail accounts; applicable bulk senders face additional requirements. LinkedIn's User Agreement restricts unauthorized automated methods, including automated messaging. Verify that your intended integration and usage are authorized before enabling social automation. Do not try to evade platform restrictions.

Privacy and legal review

For US commercial email, the FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide sets out sender responsibilities including truthful identification and subject lines, required disclosures and an effective opt-out process. Outsourcing execution does not eliminate the sender's responsibilities.

For EU personal data, review the GDPR's lawful-processing, transparency and objection requirements, including Article 21 for direct marketing. National electronic marketing rules and channel-specific laws may impose additional restrictions. Obtain jurisdiction-specific legal advice before launch; this guide does not establish that any listed product or campaign is legally compliant.

Ask each vendor for its data processing agreement, subprocessor list, security assurance, retention and deletion terms, transfer safeguards, model-training policy, export format and incident notification process. Ask which controls are included in the exact contracted plan. Record how opt-outs propagate across the agent, CRM and other authorized channels, without requiring the prospect to opt out repeatedly.

Recommended operating architecture

Use a controlled flow rather than letting the agent act on every retrieved instruction:

Approved ICP + exclusions → permitted lead data → evidence-backed research
→ policy and suppression check → human-approved action where required
→ authorized execution → reply classification → human handoff
→ verified CRM state + auditable outcome

External webpages and incoming replies are untrusted inputs, not authority to override company rules. Limit CRM writes to necessary fields, make permissions explicit and store the minimum evidence needed to investigate failures. Redact personal information from logs where possible. For trace and incident design, see SXF's observability field guide.

How to choose AI agents for sales

Founder-led B2B sales: Begin with a defined ICP and a human-supervised tool. RegieGO and Apollo merit evaluation when research and drafting are the bottleneck. Consider a usage-based outbound agent such as AiSDR when the sales motion and buyer qualification criteria are already understood.

Outbound agency: Evaluate Agent Frank, AiSDR and Amplemarket for different workflow models. Require separate client data, campaign permissions, auditable opt-outs, exportability and a quote reflecting the number of workspaces and people contacted. Confirm client isolation rather than assuming it exists.

Established revenue organization: Compare Artisan and 11x when the business wants to delegate a larger portion of outbound. A written pilot, security review, contract exit terms and verified CRM controls should precede a substantial commitment.

Inbound-led SaaS: Evaluate Qualified Piper when website demand is already present. Compare it with inbound-specific offerings rather than interpreting its results as evidence about cold-outbound prospecting.

Sales and marketing operations: Amplemarket or Apollo may fit teams that need shared data and engagement infrastructure. For broader campaign orchestration, refer to SXF's AI agents for marketing guide rather than buying a second platform with overlapping capabilities.

Before procurement, agree on the intended workload, the permitted channels, the data and review policy, acceptance criteria and a total-cost ceiling. Start in a no-send test environment; authorize a limited live pilot only after critical controls pass and relevant privacy and channel rules are satisfied. Compare against your existing human-led process using consistent qualification definitions. Record failed outcomes as well as successes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI SDR tool for 2027?

There is no single evidence-backed winner. AiSDR is a usage-based outbound candidate, Agent Frank offers selectable operation modes, Artisan and 11x target more extensive delegated workflows, RegieGO and Apollo support sales representatives, Amplemarket combines data and engagement, and Qualified focuses on inbound conversion. Choose based on the sales motion and verified pilot results.

How much does AI SDR software cost?

Public examples range from free or low-priced assisted prospecting subscriptions to annual contracts costing tens of thousands of dollars. AiSDR lists $1.50 per person prospected; 11x starts at $36,000 annually. These figures buy different services. Include human review, infrastructure, data and setup before comparing total costs.

Can an AI SDR replace a human SDR?

It can automate portions of research, drafting, follow-up, qualification and scheduling. It does not automatically replace judgment, relationship building, policy ownership, complex objections or responsibility for mistakes. A team should first measure whether the system reduces work while maintaining accepted outcomes.

What is the difference between an AI SDR and an AI sales agent?

AI SDR usually refers to early sales development and qualification. AI sales agent is broader and can include inbound conversion, account expansion or other stages of the sales cycle. Product labels overlap, so examine the actual actions and permissions.

Can AI SDR tools automate LinkedIn and email?

Some providers document these features, but capability does not imply authorization. Check the channel's rules and the applicable law; use only authorized mechanisms and respect recipient preferences. Keep sending permissions and suppression controls separate from draft generation.

How should AI sales automation success be measured?

Track qualified held meetings, opportunity quality, accepted pipeline, accuracy of targeting and personalization, oversight time, suppression violations and cost per accepted outcome. Do not use message volume or booked meetings alone as proof of success.

Are free AI SDR plans enough?

Free plans can be useful for evaluating research, drafts and workflow fit. They may have limited credits or may exclude a vendor's autonomous agent. A free trial of the base platform is not proof that the complete agent is available at no cost.
