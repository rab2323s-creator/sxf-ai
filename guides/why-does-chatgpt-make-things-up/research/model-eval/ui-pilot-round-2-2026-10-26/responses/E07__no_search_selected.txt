No, NASA's New Horizons spacecraft did not land on Pluto in 2015.

Instead, it performed a flyby of Pluto on July 14, 2015, passing approximately 12,500 kilometers (7,800 miles) above its surface.

During the flyby, New Horizons captured detailed photographs and collected scientific data about Pluto's surface, atmosphere, and moons.

The mission revealed remarkable features, including Pluto's famous heart-shaped region, known as Tombaugh Regio.

New Horizons was a flyby mission, not a landing mission. As of 2026, no spacecraft has ever landed on Pluto.
