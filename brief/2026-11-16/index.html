Google Antigravity /teamwork Uses Gemini 4 Agents to Verify Math Proofs and Optimize Code

Google Research reports human-supervised Gemini 4 multi-agent workflows tackling mathematical proofs, chemistry and major systems-engineering projects, with independent verifiers.

Google Research published new results on November 13 from Antigravity's /teamwork system, which organizes multiple Gemini 4-based agents into long-running research and engineering workflows. Rather than asking one chatbot to produce an answer, the platform divides a problem among specialized workers, auditors and verifiers. Google describes results spanning formal mathematical proofs, computational chemistry, Rust migrations and TPU performance tuning. These are substantial claims about agentic AI because the work involves creating and testing artifacts that can be evaluated outside a language model's own judgment. The crucial distinction is between human-directed collaborations that produce checkable outputs and a model independently discovering every result.

AI Research & Multi-Agent Systems: verified source facts

  • The Google Research post names Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon and Hydrogen among models used in collaborations, and says researchers set constraints and evaluate outputs alongside specialized agents.
  • One reported collaboration tackled the Courtade–Kumar information-theory conjecture using a combination of human insights, interval-arithmetic certificates and extensive Lean 4 formalization. Google reports 50 million lines of generated Lean code for the full formal proof.
  • For systems engineering, the post describes a C++-to-Rust migration of the libgav1 AV1 decoder with broad bit-exact conformance tests, plus human-audited molecular-structure and TPU-kernel results. Some new specialized patterns are still planned for external release.
Read more: SXF editorial analysis ＋

How teams can test multi-agent research responsibly

Start with a problem that has an objective checker: an unmodified software test suite, Lean proofs, a physical simulator or benchmark measurements. Divide the task into narrow agents with minimal file and tool access, and let a verifier use fresh context so it does not inherit the producer's rationale. Track all intermediate artifacts, including compiler failures, rejected hypotheses and evaluation configurations. Run an untouched holdout test after agents believe the problem is solved. For research claims, preserve a clear human contribution log and make certificates or code available for third-party reproduction. A very large generated proof is only useful if its definitions and formal assumptions match the original theorem.

SXF analysis: evidence chains matter more than agent count

The striking development is the effort to combine search, generation and falsification in an auditable workflow. That can turn model output into material experts may verify, especially where formal tools offer unambiguous feedback. It does not remove the need for mathematicians, security reviewers or domain scientists. Google describes collaborations and selected outcomes, not a universal success rate across unknown research problems. Organizations should compare completed verified tasks, engineering cost and human review hours against established approaches before funding autonomous agent fleets. The opportunity is strongest where tests are meaningful and the failure modes can be constrained.

What the evidence does not yet establish

Google's reported breakthroughs and performance numbers come from the researchers involved; publication in a company blog does not establish independent replication of every result. The post says some features are available in Antigravity now while additional advanced workflows will arrive later. This November 13 report is distinct from SXF's earlier coverage of individual Gemini model releases and older Antigravity chemistry skills.

02 / LOCAL LLMS & OPEN-WEIGHT MODELSSOURCE PUBLISHED: 2026-11-12

Edge0 Releases 145B Local AI Model With Sparse MoE Inference and Restricted Model License

Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B bring downloadable sparse mixture-of-experts weights to local coding workloads, but licensing and NVIDIA hardware requirements need scrutiny.

Edge0 announced Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B on November 12, presenting large mixture-of-experts language models designed for local coding, reasoning and agent tasks. The headline 145-billion-parameter size is not a measure of what runs on the GPU at each token: the company says roughly ten billion parameters are active during an inference step. Its architecture keeps frequently used experts close to the accelerator and streams other weights through system memory and SSD. The launch deserves attention among developers seeking private or offline inference, but model downloads, practical hardware compatibility and licenses matter more than the tempting phrase '145B on a laptop.'

Local LLMs & Open-Weight Models: verified source facts

  • Edge0's launch post reports 145 billion total parameters, about ten billion active per inference step, and an internal benchmark peak of 29 GB memory with more than 80 decoded tokens per second on a compatible setup.
  • The official Edge0 GitHub repository identifies the 145B and 120B artifacts as Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-derived models running through edge0-strata, with single-NVIDIA-GPU Linux or Windows deployment described for the first release.
  • Model weights have separate Edge0 Community Model License and upstream Qwen terms. The inference framework's open-source license does not automatically permit redistributing the model as a commercial API or product; users must review the model-specific terms.
Read more: SXF editorial analysis ＋

What the local LLM hardware claim actually means

Do not confuse peak accelerator-memory use with the total model storage or system requirements. Before downloading, check quantized weight size, SSD space, RAM headroom, NVIDIA GPU compatibility, CUDA version, driver support and documented engine versions. Run a minimal local coding prompt and then record prefill speed, generation tokens per second, peak GPU and host memory, disk activity and power usage on the exact machine. Compare answers and tool calls against a small, verified evaluation set. When testing privacy, confirm telemetry and network behavior rather than assuming a local launch guarantees that no external service is contacted.

SXF analysis: local capability depends on the complete stack

This release signals continuing demand for models that can work without sending source code to a hosted provider. Sparse expert designs and tiered memory can make larger parameter counts technically feasible on constrained hardware, but performance is highly dependent on data transfer, cache hit rates and software optimization. Edge0's internal numbers do not prove broadly faster inference than cloud alternatives, and its model terms limit some commercial deployments. For organizations exploring open-weight AI, reproducible benchmarks and legal clearance are essential; treating the runtime license as the weight license would be a costly error.

What the evidence does not yet establish

The 29 GB and throughput figures are vendor-reported internal measurements, not universal laptop requirements or independently audited results. Some public media reports describe macOS support for earlier Edge0 engines; those should not be assumed to apply to these newly released 145B artifacts. Separate permissions may be needed for commercial model services, and upstream Qwen obligations still apply.

03 / GEMINI & PERSONAL AISOURCE PUBLISHED: 2026-11-13

Google Gemini Adds Google Wallet and Plaid Account Data for Read-Only AI Finance Insights

US Gemini users can opt in to Google Wallet-connected financial context, with Plaid account linking and read-only insights rather than money movement.

Google expanded Personal Intelligence in the Gemini app to include Google Wallet financial context on November 13. Its partner Plaid confirmed that eligible US users can connect banking, card, loan and investment information through Wallet to receive more personalized spending and budgeting answers. This shifts a general-purpose assistant from discussing hypothetical budgets toward interpreting a user's actual transactions. It is a major privacy and consumer-product milestone even though the system is explicitly read-only. The story should not be confused with the separately announced ChatGPT Wallet, which SXF covered in its November 15 edition and which concerns purchasing with a user approval step.

Gemini & Personal AI: verified source facts

  • Plaid's November 13 announcement says users may choose to link bank accounts, credit cards, loans and investment accounts through Google Wallet for personalized Gemini responses in the United States.
  • The partner says Wallet is rolling out as a connected app inside Gemini Personal Intelligence, and people can turn the connection on or off in settings or manage their Plaid connections through Plaid Portal.
  • Plaid explicitly describes Gemini's access as read-only: the assistant cannot move, manage or invest funds. Its examples include visualizing spending, spotting possible savings and planning a major expense.
Read more: SXF editorial analysis ＋

How to evaluate read-only finance assistants

An eligible user should inspect the permissions displayed during linking, verify which accounts are visible and start with a narrowly scoped question such as monthly subscriptions. Cross-check generated totals against bank statements, especially around refunds, transfers, pending charges, currencies and duplicate transactions. Never interpret an automatically generated chart as an audited financial record. Developers creating a similar experience should treat financial data as sensitive, minimize fields passed to language models, explain the retention and revocation policy and require explicit consent for each data source. Read-only restrictions should be enforced by the underlying authorization scopes, not only by chatbot instructions.

SXF analysis: personalization becomes a data-governance problem

Financial context can make consumer AI more useful, but usefulness depends on correct categorization and on users understanding which financial accounts are linked. This is distinct from autonomous agentic commerce: answering a spending question is not placing an order, and reading a balance is not transferring money. The product may help users identify recurring costs, but it introduces new risks if they trust incorrect summaries or connect more sensitive information than needed. Transparent scope controls and clear error handling will matter more than the novelty of chatting about money.

What the evidence does not yet establish

The official partner announcement does not establish global access, coverage of every institution, guaranteed transaction accuracy or financial-advice suitability. Plaid's survey statistics are not independent evidence that this specific feature improves financial outcomes. Claims about automatic investments or transfers would contradict the read-only limitation described by the partner.

Cognition Launches Devin Apps for Private Agent-Built Internal Tools and Preview Deployments

Devin Apps lets teams create and share authenticated web tools hosted on Devin's cloud machine, with access to existing code and integrations.

Cognition introduced Devin Apps on November 13, extending its Devin coding agent beyond writing pull requests into hosting working internal applications on the same cloud computer used for development. Teams can ask Devin to build a dashboard, an internal operations interface or a preview deployment and then share the result through an authenticated link. Cognition describes an approach in which existing code, connected tools and integration context remain available to the running app. This makes the story particularly relevant to developers using agentic AI to ship working software, rather than just collect generated snippets. It is an application-hosting feature, not a new underlying foundation model.

Coding Agents & Automation: verified source facts

  • Cognition's November 13 announcement describes private shareable web applications that execute on Devin's cloud machine and can draw on local code, existing components and configured tools.
  • The company says Devin Apps are intended for internal tooling, preview deployments and extensions of Devin itself, with user authentication rather than public anonymous access.
  • Cognition describes memory-snapshot-based hibernation and wake-up behavior to reduce idle compute. Its own examples include internal reporting dashboards and maintaining applications through subsequent agent sessions.
Read more: SXF editorial analysis ＋

How to deploy an internal tool without exposing production secrets

Start with a disposable data source and least-privilege service credentials. Verify which teammates can open the app, whether links are organization-scoped and whether users share the developer's underlying integrations. Treat an AI-generated internal app as production software if it can query financial systems, support tickets or customer records. Run automated tests for access control, injection risks, data segregation and audit-log coverage. Pin dependencies, review database permissions and establish a rollback path for every update. Compare total runtime and agent maintenance costs with your existing preview-hosting platform, including what happens when a dormant app wakes after a long idle period.

SXF analysis: the agent runtime becomes an application platform

Devin Apps reduces the distance between implementing a feature and letting colleagues use it. The strategic change is control of the full loop: the agent can create, host, observe and revise a working service within its environment. For small teams, that may lower the overhead of internal tools; for enterprises, coupling development, execution and connected credentials also concentrates risk. Authentication is a necessary safeguard but not proof of tenant isolation or compliance with every data policy. Evaluate real task completion, change review and recoverability rather than assuming an agent-created application is maintenance-free.

What the evidence does not yet establish

The vendor's description of private hosting is not a third-party penetration test or an independent guarantee that all integrations are safe. Availability and billing can differ across Devin plans and organizations; verify current entitlement before estimating costs. This is separate from SXF's earlier stories about Claude and Gemini model availability inside coding products.

05 / AI AGENTS & DEVELOPER INFRASTRUCTURESOURCE PUBLISHED: 2026-11-13

LangChain Raises $300 Million at $3 Billion Valuation to Expand LangSmith AI Agent Platform

LangChain reports a $300M Series C led by CapitalG, valuing the business at $3B, as LangSmith adds agent runtime, evaluations and governance.

LangChain announced a $300 million Series C funding round on November 13 at a stated $3 billion valuation. The news is less about investor excitement than about the infrastructure challenges created by enterprise AI agents. LangChain started as a framework for connecting language models to tools and data; it now positions LangSmith as a broader system for running, evaluating and governing applications that take actions. With companies deploying multiple agent systems, the market is increasingly interested in traceability, deployment controls and measurable reliability. A major financing round supports the direction but does not by itself prove software quality or revenue sustainability.

AI Agents & Developer Infrastructure: verified source facts

  • LangChain says CapitalG led the round with participation from GIC, DFJ Growth, IVP and Sapphire Ventures. The company's announcement states the $300 million amount and $3 billion valuation.
  • The company reports more than 7,000 organizations using LangSmith and cites customers using agent traces, evaluations and governance; these are company-provided adoption figures.
  • LangChain divides its LangSmith strategy into Agent Runtime, Agent Improvement and Agent Governance, including observability, evaluation, sandboxes, gateways, model budgets and tool-access controls. It announced a further product event for November 20.
Read more: SXF editorial analysis ＋

What an enterprise agent platform should prove

Engineering teams should test an agent-management platform against reproducible tasks rather than its funding headline. Instrument every tool call with a trace ID, measure success and human escalation rates and maintain a holdout dataset for regression testing. Evaluate whether the runtime supports isolation of secrets, constrained outbound network access, per-tenant policy enforcement and model-provider failover. Review whether stored traces capture private information and whether retention can be controlled. If portability is a priority, test exporting workflows, datasets and metrics and running the same evaluation against another model or orchestration framework.

SXF analysis: agent observability is moving into the core software stack

The funding points to a strategic bet that businesses will want control over the data and workflows that make AI agents useful, even when the models themselves are interchangeable. The opportunity is credible: an agent that makes costly mistakes needs better inspection and remediation than a simple chat interface. But a large financing round cannot settle the competitive landscape. Major AI vendors and cloud platforms now offer overlapping runtimes and monitoring. LangChain must demonstrate that its architecture reduces total deployment and debugging costs without creating new lock-in. Enterprises should ask how the platform behaves under failure, not only how quickly a demonstration comes together.

What the evidence does not yet establish

Valuation and customer counts are reported by LangChain and should not be confused with independently verified profitability, revenue or market share. The company has announced a November 20 event; do not describe its future features as already shipped. No general-purpose agent benchmark improvement follows automatically from a funding announcement.
