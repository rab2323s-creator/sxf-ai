SXF ORIGINAL BENCHMARK ANALYSIS

Open TTS Leaderboard: what the open-source speech rankings actually tell you.

Source snapshot: September 30, 2026

Looking for an open-source TTS leaderboard? Hugging Face launched the Open TTS Leaderboard to compare text-to-speech models using repeatable measurements—not just one overall popularity score. It evaluates intelligibility, synthesis throughput, streaming responsiveness and, when supported, reference-speaker similarity across English and other languages.

SXF's analysis: the useful output is a shortlist of models for your specific application, not a universal winner. A low word error rate does not guarantee a natural voice; a fast batched model does not necessarily start speaking quickly; and an English leader may not be the right choice for another language. The models below are those the original September 30, 2026 announcement highlighted. For current rankings, use the live leaderboard.

LEADERS AT THE SEPTEMBER 2026 LAUNCH

Which TTS models stood out, and for what?

ENGLISH / INTELLIGIBILITY

Kokoro-82M, Supertonic-3, Fish Audio S2 Pro.

Hugging Face highlighted Kokoro-82M, Supertonic-3 and Fish Audio S2 Pro among English word-error-rate leaders across Seed TTS Eval and CV3 Eval. The original post does not establish one permanent best TTS model across all criteria.

MULTILINGUAL VOICES

OmniVoice, Fish Audio S2 Pro, CosyVoice3.

The original analysis highlighted OmniVoice, Fish Audio S2 Pro and Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B-2512 on multilingual tests. Your target language, accent, segmentation rules and voice settings can change the comparison.

STREAMING / GPU + CPU

Kyutai Pocket TTS.

Hugging Face calls out Pocket TTS as a strong streaming option on GPU and CPU. For real-time agents, inspect the TTFA tab on your actual serving hardware instead of relying only on WER or batched generation speed.

VOICE CLONING / SPEAKER SIMILARITY

Evaluate conditioned voices separately.

The Voice cloning toggle exposes models that accept a reference voice and adds a speaker-similarity measurement. Hugging Face notes improvements in WER for some models, including Higgs TTS 3 and VoxCPM2, when reference audio is provided. A voice-cloning subset must not be compared blindly with unconditioned synthesis.

Ranking caveat: these are models highlighted in the launch article, not a reproduced live top-five table. We deliberately do not invent exact positions, WER scores or today's rankings. The leaderboard can add models, datasets and new results after the announcement.

HOW TO READ THE SCORES

WER, CER, RTFx, TTFA and SIM: what each metric measures.

MetricWhat it tells youBetter directionWhat it cannot prove
WER · word error rateHow often ASR transcription of the generated speech differs from prompt words.Lower ↓Naturalness, accent fit or emotional expression.
CER · character error rateCharacter-level transcription disagreement, used for certain languages and scripts.Lower ↓Human preference or fair comparison across unlike languages.
RTFx · inverse real-time factorHow much audio is synthesized per unit of inference time in the batched test.Higher ↑Low latency for the first audio chunk.
TTFA · time to first audioWait time before audio can begin playback in the streaming-focused test.Lower ↓Tail latency, concurrency or whole-response quality.
SIM · speaker similarityCosine similarity between WavLM embeddings of generated and reference voices.Higher ↑Prosody, consent, or a complete measure of voice likeness.

A crucial measurement distinction: the leaderboard uses Qwen3 ASR to estimate synthesized speech intelligibility. That makes recognition technology an evaluation instrument; it does not turn these numbers into a standalone STT model ranking. See SXF's Open ASR leaderboard coverage for the speech-recognition side of the ecosystem.

BENCHMARK METHODOLOGY

Why language, dataset, hardware and voice conditions matter.

01 / DEFAULT RANK

English WER is not multilingual quality. The initial default view uses the macro-average English WER across Seed TTS Eval and CV3 Eval (zero-shot). It ranks a specific benchmark slice, not every language or accent.

02 / MULTILINGUAL

Non-English data is not identical across datasets. Seed TTS Eval contains speech for English and Chinese in this setup. Other evaluated languages rely on the CV3 Eval zero-shot scores. The leaderboard reports CER for Chinese, Japanese and Korean and uses a macro-average across selected languages. Always inspect which languages and datasets contribute.

03 / STREAMING

TTFA has a reproducible test boundary. Hugging Face uses 50 English prompts from CV3 Eval, batch size one and each model's default voice. It discards the first three warm-up runs and reports median TTFA. H200 GPU results are the default; CPU results cover a smaller subset. For non-streaming models the clock runs until the full utterance is ready.

04 / VOICE CLONING

Reference audio changes the task. Enable the voice-cloning filter only for models and languages that support it. Inspect WavLM-based SIM alongside WER and listen to examples; evaluate consent and authorized use separately from technical similarity.

05 / MODEL SIZE

One best score may conceal a production trade-off. The leaderboard's Pareto views compare intelligibility, throughput and model size, while its Listen tab supports direct audio comparison. Also measure RAM/VRAM, deployment license and sustained concurrent traffic on your own stack.

STT VS TTS VS SPEECH-TO-SPEECH

Which speech leaderboard should you use?

TEXT → AUDIO

Text-to-speech · TTS.

Use the Open TTS Leaderboard to compare generated speech, intelligibility, voice cloning and playback responsiveness. It is the focus of this article.

AUDIO → TEXT

Speech-to-text · STT / ASR.

Use the Open ASR Leaderboard for speech transcription recognition tests. A TTS model's output may be evaluated by ASR, but the objectives are different.

AUDIO → AUDIO

Speech-to-speech · S2S.

A complete conversational audio system may contain recognition, reasoning, translation and synthesis, or a direct audio-to-audio model. End-to-end latency and conversational quality cannot be inferred from a TTS leaderboard alone.

SXF PRACTICAL SELECTION FRAMEWORK

How to choose a TTS model from the leaderboard.

01

Pick the real use case: narration, accessibility, multilingual content, a low-latency voice agent or authorized voice cloning. Define a minimum acceptable intelligibility threshold and an audio delay budget.

02

Filter the correct evaluation slice: choose language, dataset and optional voice cloning; use the Streaming tab for interactive applications rather than choosing from default English WER alone.

03

Shortlist three or four candidates: check low WER/CER, low TTFA when relevant, RTFx for bulk generation and the memory footprint or model size you can host.

04

Listen blind: have domain speakers assess naturalness, pronunciation, pauses, expression and named entities on your own prompts. Objective WER cannot capture all of these attributes.

05

Test production hardware: reproduce the serving precision, CPU/GPU hardware, concurrency and streaming settings. Capture median and slower-request latency rather than extrapolating from a single H200 run.

06

Verify permission and provenance: inspect model weights, license, dataset and voice-cloning rights, and record the exact model revision and evaluation date before shipping.

SXF's interpretation: open-weight availability and published benchmarks make it easier to shortlist models, but do not automatically imply commercial-use permission, a common runtime cost or proven accessibility across every supported language. The right outcome is a testable procurement decision, not an SEO-friendly but misleading “best TTS overall” label.

COMMON SEARCH QUESTIONS

Open-source TTS leaderboard FAQ.

01

What is the Open TTS Leaderboard? It is a Hugging Face leaderboard for comparing open text-to-speech models across intelligibility, generation speed, multilingual performance and optional voice cloning. The live leaderboard is hosted at huggingface.co/spaces/hf-audio/open_tts_leaderboard.

02

Which open-source TTS models led the English evaluation at launch? In its September 30, 2026 announcement, Hugging Face highlighted Kokoro-82M, Supertonic-3 and Fish Audio S2 Pro among the leaders on English macro-average WER. The interactive rankings can change, so this is not a permanent numbered ranking.

03

Which TTS models were strong for multilingual synthesis? Hugging Face highlighted OmniVoice, Fish Audio S2 Pro and Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B-2512 as strong multilingual models in its announcement. Switch to the intended language and dataset before comparing.

04

What does WER mean in a TTS leaderboard? Word error rate measures how often an ASR system's transcript of synthesized speech disagrees with the requested text. Lower WER generally indicates better intelligibility for that benchmark, not necessarily more natural or expressive audio.

05

Why does the leaderboard report CER for some languages? For languages such as Chinese, Japanese and Korean, character error rate is more suitable than word error rate for the leaderboard's reported evaluations. Comparisons must account for segmentation, script and dataset differences.

06

What is time-to-first-audio (TTFA)? TTFA measures the delay before playable audio becomes available. The Hugging Face streaming evaluation reports median TTFA at batch size 1 after three warm-up runs on 50 English prompts, with H200 GPU and limited CPU results.

07

What is RTFx in text-to-speech benchmarking? Inverse real-time factor is a throughput measure: higher RTFx indicates generating more seconds of audio per second of inference under the reported setup. Batch throughput does not by itself measure first-audio latency.

08

How does the leaderboard evaluate voice cloning? When supported, the leaderboard measures speaker embedding cosine similarity (SIM) using WavLM embeddings between generated audio and a reference voice. Higher SIM is a proxy for identity preservation, not a complete judgment of quality or permission to clone a person's voice.

09

Is the Open TTS Leaderboard also an STT or speech-to-speech leaderboard? No. TTS converts text into speech. The Open ASR Leaderboard measures speech recognition (STT). A speech-to-speech system includes additional components, so a TTS-only score cannot rank full speech-to-speech systems.

10

Does the best WER model also sound the most human? Not necessarily. WER/CER are intelligibility proxies. Naturalness, prosody, emotion, accent fit and listener preference require listening tests; Hugging Face offers a Listen tab for samples and feedback.

11

Is Pocket TTS best for realtime or CPU speech synthesis? Hugging Face specifically highlighted Kyutai Pocket TTS for strong streaming performance on GPU and CPU in the September 30 announcement. The best deployment choice still depends on target hardware, concurrency and the current TTFA table.

12

How should a team choose an open-source TTS model? Filter the live leaderboard by language, voice cloning and streaming requirements, compare WER or CER alongside TTFA, RTFx and size, check the actual model license, then test a few candidates on private representative prompts and target hardware.
