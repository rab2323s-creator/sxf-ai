Microsoft 365 outage hits Outlook, Teams and Copilot worldwide: latest confirmed updates

A Microsoft 365 incident disrupted Outlook mail access, Exchange Online and some Teams and Copilot features across regions on November 12–13, 2026. Reports came from 74 countries and territories; Microsoft's recovery updates showed gradual improvements, not an immediate all-clear.

What happened: A widespread Microsoft 365 service disruption on Thursday, November 12, affected users trying to access Outlook mailboxes and other Exchange Online-dependent workflows. The consequences spread to some Microsoft Teams, Microsoft 365 Copilot, SharePoint, security and administration features. By Friday morning, Microsoft had implemented traffic-management and server-stability measures, but its own incident report had not declared a full restoration.

Is Outlook down today? What users experienced

The most visible symptom was disruption to Exchange Online, the cloud email platform behind work and school Outlook accounts. Microsoft said affected customers might be unable to open their mailbox through Outlook on the web, desktop MAPI connections, Exchange ActiveSync or REST-based integrations. Messages could also be delayed even when a client appeared to reconnect.

Downdetector recorded a surge of public reports. Reuters reported approximately 138,058 Outlook reports around 1 p.m. US Eastern on November 12, falling to roughly 34,490 by around 6 p.m. Eastern. These are report submissions, not a deduplicated count of affected people, and the number does not quantify all enterprise tenants or infrastructure failures.

Reports were logged from 74 countries and territories within about an hour, according to Downdetector data cited by Euronews. That supports a geographically widespread disruption, but not a precise estimate of lost productivity, impacted customers or the number of devices offline.

Which Microsoft services were affected—and what still worked?

Microsoft's status notice distinguishes services directly blocked from those affected only when they need Exchange data. The difference matters to a user searching “Teams down” or “Copilot not working”: a disrupted mailbox does not necessarily mean every part of Microsoft 365 is unavailable.

What caused the Microsoft 365 outage?

Microsoft initially described elevated infrastructure load in multiple regions and servers failing automated health checks. Its subsequent investigation found that many servers had become unexpectedly unavailable, concentrating traffic on the remaining capacity. In other words, the elevated load was not necessarily evidence of a sudden wave of legitimate users; the effective capacity of the service had dropped.

Later updates described an overloaded process involved in establishing secure connections on affected machines. Microsoft explored traffic isolation, restarts, rollback of a recent network configuration change and targeted patches. By the morning of November 13, Microsoft reported improving server stability after deploying a patch associated with an upstream authentication/routing configuration component, then expanded it in phases.

The final root cause was not confirmed at the reviewed cutoff. Microsoft said it had no evidence of malicious activity associated with the incident and intended to publish a post-incident review. A suspected configuration correlation is not the same as a completed root-cause investigation, so describing the outage as a confirmed cyberattack—or attributing it definitively to one software update—would go beyond the evidence.

SXF analysis: why Copilot and AI connectors were vulnerable

SXF / AI · ORIGINAL ANALYSIS

AI models can be operational while the data layer around them fails.

The Microsoft 365 interruption illustrates a dependency that ordinary “AI assistant uptime” measurements can miss. Copilot experiences and third-party AI tools often need fresh business context—mail messages, calendars, documents and permissions—before a useful answer can be generated. When an upstream identity, messaging or Microsoft Graph pathway fails, a model may still compute text while the assistant cannot retrieve the data needed to complete the user's request.

Reuters reported that Microsoft 365 connectors used by OpenAI and Anthropic were also disrupted. OpenAI was investigating broken new connector connections and tools not working as expected; Anthropic identified an upstream provider issue affecting its Microsoft 365 connector. These reports do not establish a general outage of ChatGPT or Claude's underlying models.

For enterprise AI deployments, the practical failure domains form a chain: assistant interface → identity and permissions → connector/API → enterprise data source → model response. An incident at any dependency can make the whole workflow appear broken. Incident dashboards and vendor service-level objectives should therefore include tool-call success, data freshness and dependency health—not only whether the model endpoint returns an answer.

The same reasoning applies to Microsoft Graph automations, workflow engines and agents that synchronize mail or create meetings. An application can return a successful HTTP response from one layer while its business action is delayed or blocked downstream. Business impact: why a cloud email incident spread so widely

A cloud collaboration suite concentrates everyday operational dependencies: customer support inboxes, calendars, internal scheduling, identity workflows, security controls and automation connectors. Even partial loss of Exchange Online can disrupt activity beyond email. Associated Press reported effects across government, healthcare, travel and other organizations as the disruption extended into Friday.

The effect is not identical for every customer. An organization whose critical meetings and documentation remained accessible may have had continuity, while another whose work depends on mailbox APIs, calendar scheduling and Copilot work-data access could face cascading failures. The key difference is dependency architecture, not just the logo on the affected app.

For an incident review, teams should measure unavailable minutes by workload, delayed mail delivery, failed task executions, recovery queue length and reliance on alternate communication channels. No verified global estimate of economic losses or affected individuals was available in the sources reviewed, so SXF does not assign a speculative dollar figure.

What users and IT administrators can do during the outage

  1. Check the official status. Admins should track MO1550821 and EX1550543 in the Microsoft 365 admin center or Microsoft service health. A regional success report is not proof every tenant has recovered.
  2. Use independent communication channels. If email or Teams calendar invites fail, coordinate urgent work via your organization's approved phone or alternate communication platform.
  3. Avoid duplicate and destructive actions. Treat queued email, calendar invitations and automated operations as potentially delayed. Repeating sends or workflow actions without checking delivery state can create duplicates after recovery.
  4. Check integrations separately. Microsoft Graph, third-party connectors and AI assistants may recover at different times. Verify authorizations, new connector connections and retry/error queues after the upstream incident improves.
  5. Protect security procedures. If Defender or Purview features are degraded, use approved escalation procedures; do not disable protections or bypass compliance policies simply to restore convenience.
  6. Record the post-incident outcome. After Microsoft issues its promised post-incident report, compare the final root cause with today's preliminary explanations, then update business continuity and vendor-dependency plans.

Microsoft 365 outage: frequently asked questions

Is Outlook still down on November 13, 2026?

The last Microsoft advisory reviewed by SXF, at 09:57 UTC on November 13, still classified the wider incident as service degradation. Microsoft described incremental recovery but variable capacity. Verify the live status for your own region or organization.

Were all Teams meetings and chats down?

No. Microsoft reported that most Teams calls, meetings and ordinary messages continued working. Calendar, scheduling, invitations and other Exchange-dependent functionality were among the affected features.

Was Microsoft Copilot affected?

Yes. Microsoft listed issues with Copilot Chat and work-data access in some Microsoft 365 experiences, while separate reports identified problems with OpenAI and Anthropic Microsoft 365 connectors. This does not establish that underlying AI models stopped working.

What caused the Microsoft 365 outage?

Microsoft identified unexpectedly unavailable servers, an overloaded connection process and investigated authentication/routing configuration changes. It had not published a final root cause at the reviewed cutoff, and it stated that it had no evidence of malicious activity.

Does “74 countries affected” mean all users were offline?

No. Downdetector received reports from 74 countries and territories, but public report locations are not an independently audited count of affected users or full regional outages.

What should Microsoft 365 administrators monitor?

Incident IDs MO1550821 and EX1550543, mailbox availability, delayed email queues, Teams scheduling, Graph API errors, Copilot data access and connected third-party AI workflows.
