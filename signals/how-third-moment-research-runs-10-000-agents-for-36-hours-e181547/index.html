How Third Moment Research runs 10,000 agents for 36+ hours

SOURCEOpenAI
PUBLISHEDOctober 14, 2026
LAYERResearch
SIGNAL SCORE53/100

SOURCE SUMMARY

Third Moment Research says it operates 10,000 concurrent AI agents for 36–48-hour research sessions. Its OpenAI case study reports a 97% backtesting-time reduction after Codex identified caching and memory-packing optimizations; an outer agent harness handles stalled tasks, and Daybreak tests simulation security.

WHY IT MATTERS

The engineering lesson is not simply scale. Faster tool execution reduces status-polling tokens and context noise; an outer supervisor can retry, end or escalate stalled work; and security testing becomes a precondition for wider autonomy. These are separate infrastructure requirements, not proofs that every agent completes its job successfully.

WHAT TO VERIFY

OpenAI reports customer-supplied results, not an independently audited benchmark. Verify the exact concurrency window, accounting for failed and retried runs, token and compute cost per accepted result, backtest correctness, sandbox escape testing and human escalation rate before comparing the system with alternatives.

SXF ORIGINAL ANALYSIS / OCTOBER 18, 2026

Inside Third Moment's 10,000-agent research system

Source: OpenAI customer report

Running thousands of agents for days is an infrastructure problem as much as a model capability. Third Moment Research's published case study connects parallel investigations, faster backtesting, supervision, and containment into one operational loop. The numbers below are reported by OpenAI from the company's experience, not independently reproduced performance measurements by SXF.

The decisive change: remove the waiting, not just speed up the model

Agents in the quantitative-research environment were repeatedly checking whether lengthy market-data backtests had finished. Those status requests consumed tokens and filled working context without advancing the investigation. According to the OpenAI report, Codex used recorded trajectories and code-level profiling to locate a bottleneck, then introduced caching and memory-packing changes.

One backtest went from roughly three hours to roughly three to five minutes. That improvement does more than shorten a test: it gives a long-running agent quicker observations and allows the next experiment to be chosen sooner. The reported 97% reduction is specific to this customer workflow; it is not a general speedup figure for AI agents or Codex.

ENGINEERING TAKEAWAY 01Optimize the action–observation loop.

At large scale, slow tools cause repeated polling, context growth and wasted reasoning budget. Tool latency, cancellation and state management can become as important as the underlying language model.

How the system keeps agents working for 36–48 hours

OpenAI describes a Codex execution harness inside a containerized research environment with access to the documentation, code and data relevant to each task. GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6.1 Sol support reasoning and the research process, while a separate supervisory harness powered by GPT-6 Sol or GPT-6.1 Sol examines interrupted trajectories. It can choose to retry, stop the job or send it for human review rather than assuming an unfinished task is a success.

Third Moment also describes finding recurring failure patterns in recorded agent trajectories, creating targeted synthetic cases, and using those cases to improve the system. This makes failure analysis and evaluation design part of the feedback loop. It does not establish that the resulting model is universally self-improving or that a fleet of 10,000 independent runs always succeeds.

Security is part of the compute pipeline

The company uses Daybreak to test whether agents in its simulation environment could escape their sandbox and reach the surrounding network. This matters because an agent allowed to operate for days has more opportunities to encounter malicious inputs, misuse credentials or take unintended actions. A security gate before launch is sensible, but the OpenAI story does not provide public penetration-test pass rates or an independent attestation.

ENGINEERING TAKEAWAY 02Autonomy needs an independent stop condition.

A long context window alone cannot guarantee task completion. Supervision, scoped permissions, observability and a human escalation path need to sit outside the agent's immediate objective.

Which models are involved?

The report names GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6.1 Sol as reasoning components. It also describes using GPT-5.6 with Codex to diagnose the backtest bottleneck. Codex is the coding-agent harness, not a substitute name for one of these models. Third Moment says it evaluates models against its own tasks and values GPT-6.1 Sol's reported token efficiency; those are customer observations, not a cross-provider head-to-head benchmark.

COMPANY PROFILE / FUNDING

What is Third Moment Research, and is its funding public?

Third Moment Research describes an OpenAI-compatible, auto-improving inference service built around collecting execution trajectories, diagnosing recurrent errors and improving models or agent harnesses with targeted evaluations. OpenAI describes it as a North American technology startup.

Funding status: The public company website and OpenAI customer story reviewed on October 18, 2026 did not state a funding-round amount, investor list or announced valuation. This is a statement about those sources, not evidence that the company has raised no capital. We do not assign an unsupported funding figure.
